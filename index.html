Car Load Planner

Enter the cars for a trip and get a position for each one on the truck and trailer, checked against length, height, stacked height and payload.

Limits come from your 10 real Eurolohr 200 loads. Each position allows the tallest and longest car you actually put there, so they are on the safe side. Correct them under “Truck specs” at the bottom; your changes are kept in this browser. Positions show Lohr's own codes where known (B2 over-cab platform, K2 trailer upper deck); add the rest from your truck.

Cars to load

Stop 1 is the first delivery. Big and tall cars go on the upper deck. Tick “Roof box” to add the box height. Type in the model box to search 575 models (500 from the car database). All sizes are approximate: check them. Cars that are tall for their size are kept off the over-cab spot where possible.

Load plan

    Truck specs

    Max length and max height are per position. Unload order: 1 comes off first. Stacked height is the combined height of the car above and the car below on one stack, which is how the 4 m overall limit plays out in practice. Deck length is the combined length of the three cars on one trailer deck: the middle spot only fits when the cars either side leave room. Fill order decides which spots get used first when the truck is not full. Upper deck headroom is the spare height the planner tries to keep on upper positions. Lohr code is Lohr's own name for the position (B2 = over-cab platform, K2 = trailer upper deck); fill in the others from your truck. Payload defaults come from listings of 2017–18 EHR 200 C2 units (body about 6.9 t, trailer about 9.8 t); check your registration papers. Overhang in your loads is how far cars stuck out front and rear in your 10 real loads, so country rules are applied on top of that.
